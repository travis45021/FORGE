# The FORGE Constitution

Status: Approved foundational authority
Effective date: 2026-07-26
Authority: Highest governing document of the FORGE project

## Preamble

FORGE exists to empower people through trustworthy technology. It makes
advanced manufacturing understandable, reliable, accessible, adaptable, and
under the user's control. Every significant recommendation must be
evidence-backed; every significant action must be explainable; and FORGE must
act only within authority deliberately delegated to it.

## Article I - Constitutional authority

The governing hierarchy is:

1. This Constitution.
2. FORGE Architecture Specifications.
3. Engineering and API standards.
4. Implementation documentation.
5. Source code and extensions.

A lower level may not contradict a higher level. A feature that conflicts with
the Constitution must be redesigned before acceptance.

## Article II - The five pillars

1. **Evidence first.** Trust is earned by measurable, relevant evidence rather
   than brand, popularity, or unsupported claims.
2. **Explain everything significant.** User-facing explanations answer what
   happened, why, and what the user can do next.
3. **Users decide; FORGE follows.** Users choose the authority they delegate.
   Automation may advise, assist, or act only within that delegation.
4. **Accessibility by design.** FORGE provides plain-language workflows,
   sensible defaults, progressive disclosure, and expert control without
   requiring programming knowledge where an understandable interface is
   practical.
5. **Learn continuously and honestly.** FORGE may learn from verified outcomes
   and consented feedback, but never claims knowledge or confidence that the
   evidence does not support.

## Article III - Core principles

- **Intellectual honesty:** FORGE distinguishes facts, observations,
  specifications, inferences, predictions, assumptions, and unknowns.
- **User authority:** users can inspect, pause, resume, reject, revoke, and
  audit delegated automation, subject to legitimate safety and access policy.
- **AI advises through evidence:** AI identity does not grant authority. AI
  outputs are observations, confidence, alternatives, or recommendations until
  evaluated and authorized through the Executive.
- **Significant decisions are recorded:** important recommendations,
  approvals, denials, overrides, interventions, and outcomes are kept in an
  auditable Decision Ledger.
- **Capability before implementation:** FORGE works with documented behavior,
  not manufacturer-specific assumptions.
- **Missions before isolated commands:** meaningful work has context,
  objectives, verification, recovery, and a report.
- **Open and interoperable by design:** core interfaces, data, and extensions
  remain documented, portable, and replaceable where practical.

## Article IV - Hardware freedom and kernel stability

FORGE is hardware-neutral. Compatibility is determined by documented
capabilities, safe operation, and user authorization, not manufacturer,
branding, partnership, or affiliation.

The FORGE Kernel remains small and stable. New hardware, accessories, profiles,
drivers, material data, AI specialists, and workflows must be introduced
through capabilities, plugins, services, Missions, and configuration whenever
practical. A new component implementing known capabilities must not require a
kernel change.

Users must be able to define hand-built or unknown hardware through guided,
no-code configuration when its behavior can be expressed through existing
capabilities. Unvalidated hardware is provisional, not dismissed.

## Article V - Safety, security, and privacy

FORGE uses deterministic verification where it is reliable: authentication,
authorization, signatures, integrity, schema, compatibility, and permission
checks. Sentinel analyzes evidence and may recommend or apply only narrowly
declared safety or security interventions. Significant interventions are
explainable and recorded.

FORGE is local-first. Shared services, shared data, and shared compute require
clear, informed opt-in. No database, remote service, AI system, slicer, twin,
or simulation directly controls hardware without valid local authority.

## Article VI - Engineering and review

Technology is chosen for reliability, transparency, maintainability, security,
accessibility, and measurable value, not fashion. Public behavior is documented
and tested. Major services, AI systems, plugins, capability standards, and
architecture changes undergo constitutional review.

Every core file or service must have human-readable documentation of its
purpose, dependencies, effects, public contract, limitations, and extension
points before it is considered v1 release-ready.

## Article VII - Non-negotiables

FORGE does not trade user control for convenience, evidence for assumption,
transparency for opaque automation, security for compatibility, accessibility
for needless complexity, documentation for speed, or hardware freedom for
commercial interest.

## FORGE promise

FORGE will explain significant actions, communicate uncertainty honestly,
preserve user authority, welcome compatible innovation, protect local and
shared systems, and choose redesign over compromise when these commitments are
at risk.
